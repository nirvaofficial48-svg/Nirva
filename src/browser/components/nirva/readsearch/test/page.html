Field notes from the violet observatory

During the winter survey, the research team carried a quartz observatory compass across the ridge to find the sheltered valley. The violet compass was built for the narrow magnetic field near the old stone tower, where ordinary instruments drifted several degrees each hour. At dawn, a clear line of stars appeared above the eastern slope, and the team recorded the time, temperature, and direction in a paper notebook. They marked the safest path with small circles on the map, then checked each point again after the snow stopped. The observatory was quiet, but its weather instruments continued to send steady readings through the long night. In the morning, the team compared the readings with the previous week's notes and found that the ridge wind had shifted toward the coast. They packed the quartz observatory compass beside the notebook and planned another survey when the sky cleared. A second group used the same map to follow the marked path without disturbing the instruments. They found the tower door, inspected the brass scale, and wrote down how the reflected light changed before sunset. Those records helped the team understand why the violet compass stayed steady in the sheltered valley while the metal needle near the ridge continued to move. Every observation was checked twice, and the final report included the route, the weather, and the exact phrase quartz observatory compass so another reader could find the note later.
